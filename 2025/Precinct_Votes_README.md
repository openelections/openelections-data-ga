# Precinct vs. County Level Election Data & Vote Breakouts

In Georgia election datasets, **county-level files** provide certified results with complete vote method breakouts (`election_day_votes`, `advanced_votes`, `absentee_by_mail_votes`, and `provisional_votes`) for each county, but without precinct-level geographic granularity (`precinct` is marked `not available`). Conversely, **precinct-level files** capture results at the granular polling location level via the total `votes` column, which reconciles against official county totals. However, precinct data does not always arrive with vote breakouts from state and county election systems. When early, advance, or absentee voting is tabulated centrally at county election offices, or when reporting systems export only certified precinct totals, the individual modality breakout columns for many precincts remain blank or incomplete.

Additionally, election authorities may withhold or suppress granular precinct results under voter privacy rules, often accompanied by the official disclaimer: *"Some results have been hidden in the cases where not enough ballots were cast to ensure voter privacy."* In sparsely populated precincts or low-turnout contests, publishing vote breakdowns could compromise ballot secrecy by revealing how specific individuals voted. On these occasions, a precinct may be omitted entirely or display zero votes in the precinct file, even though eligible ballots were cast. Those votes remain fully tabulated within the official county totals, which is another reason why minor residual discrepancies can occasionally appear between precinct sums and county totals.

Because vote method breakouts are often unallocated or unassigned at the precinct level, analysts and researchers should **never sum precinct breakout columns** to determine countywide or districtwide modality totals, as doing so will severely undercount votes. Always rely on **county-level files** as the authoritative source for certified contest totals and vote method distributions (Election Day, Advance In-Person, Absentee by Mail, and Provisional). Reserve **precinct-level files**—specifically the `votes` column—for precinct boundary mapping, localized electoral modeling, and geographic distribution analysis.
